# 15. Presentation contract per play

**Home plate.** The shared field kit embeds home plate in the home dirt (`ParkDiamond.PathTop`, 0.38 ft). Its white playing face sits only 1/8 inch above that surface to avoid z-fighting; the authored plate is one solid chalk slab with a rounded perimeter and no separate raised rim or top layer. The missing-art slab uses the same playing-face height. Standard bases retain their raised slabs. The pentagon’s rear point is at home, exactly where the extended foul lines intersect.
Its broad edge faces the mound, its point faces the catcher, and its two diagonal edges follow the foul rays. It matches the displayed strike zone's 1.84-ft width (22.08 inches); depth matches width to retain the catcher-facing point and rear edges on the foul rays. The authored 17-inch mesh scales in X/Z to that shared footprint; the missing-art fallback uses the same outline.
Both batter's boxes stay six feet deep and narrow symmetrically from 48 to 42.92 inches, retaining the existing batter centers and six inches from the plate edge to each inner chalk centerline (four inches to the visible chalk edge). Batter stance and pitch reach stay unchanged. This layout is shared by every park, batting hand and seat count; strike judgments are unchanged.

**HUD handoff.** At contact, including hit-freeze and the home-run smash, the plate score/count and matchup cards give way immediately to a compact top-right runner diamond with outs below it. The same live display serves throws, steals and pickoffs, in Exhibition and practice, with one or two seats. YOU, switch, throw-target and item prompts remain available while their action applies. Freeze, smash and special VFX never suppress these readouts or delay their return.
When live play ends, the ordinary score/count and matchup cards return on that frame; there is no HUD timer or fade. Explicit capture/debug HUD mute still hides play overlays.

No effect draws a line connecting a fielder to the ball or a throw destination. This includes Laser and lick-catch ability effects, in every play and seat configuration. Ability mechanics remain unchanged; the moving ball keeps its short trail and chemistry tint.

For each play class the camera, the stamp, and the hold are data (`data/feel/shots.json`, `table.json`). The sim emits typed `PlayEvent`s; Unity may not infer the play from caption text. The caption itself is narrated in one place: the match raises a typed `PlayCall` (its `CallBeat` facts, in order) and `PlayNarrator.Narrate` writes the words, so `Match` holds no English and a live throw's line comes from `PlayNarrator.Verdict`. ✅ P8 (stamps):
every stamp in the table is `PlayStamp.Label(PlayEvent)` over the typed outcome — the outs made, `DefensiveFeat` (buddy jump, the wall robs, a plain JUMP, a DIVE, set at the catch), `Error`, `FieldersChoice`, `RunnerResult` — and the client calls nothing else; the contact word is `PlayStamp.ContactTell` over the typed zone and the release tell is MAX / Nice! alone. ✅: one tell per live event, when it happens, at a named `BroadcastHud` anchor — never a center card.
A catch stamps OUT (or the catch word) at the glove; a force or tag stamps OUT at that bag; a runner who crosses stamps SCORE at the plate; SAFE and ERROR stay the small mid-play stickers. `PlayStamp.ShowsAtTime` is the dead-play card (counts, hits, homers); a fly, a grounder, or a caught stealing already named itself live. ✅ P0:
`PlayOutcome` carries the outs made (type, bag, runner, fielder), every runner placement, the batter's bag, error, and fielder's choice; `InPlay.ThrowToBag` decides a `ThrowVerdict` and captions are narrated from it last (`InPlay.Narrate`). No rule reads caption text.

✅ (cameras), D14: the in-play beat is `PlayCamera.LiveBeat` over typed live state — steals and pickoffs use ordinary live ball-follow, a home run smashes at the crack, every other hit holds the SET shot for `contactCutSeconds` (0.42, `table.json`, the §8.2 cut), then the close play, the rundown, and the class read off the typed hit (`AtBatResult.Class`). **An ordinary throw is not a beat**:
the follow stays on the ball; chemistry tints that ball, never a predicted line through the dirt. `PlayCamera.LiveFraming` translates the named shot onto the bag, the batter, or the dirt under the ball and carries the shot's `blend` (`Framing.Blend`; 0 is a cut, which the rig honors on the live path), and `PlayCamera.CameraHold` keeps a target (a beat, and a close-play bag) for `cameraHoldSeconds` (0.25, `table.json`) so a flicker does not re-aim (`CameraDirector.Live`;
the client owns no Vector3). A routine grounder is SET, one cut, one follow, SET. ✅: a liner cuts to `diamond-line` (same 45°, between hopper and fly), never `diamond-fly` or `diamond-grounder`. ✅ (bodies): `PlayOutcome.Bodies` is every glove and live runner where it stood at Time (`LivePlaySystem.BodiesNow`, read before the field resets); the result beat draws those, so the catching fielder stays where the catch happened and a third out does not swap the defense on screen.
✅ (HUD): the mini diamond draws every live runner at `Runner.Pip` — the segment from the last bag toward the next and the fraction run along it (`Baserunning.PathPip`), the batter-runner included, a return as the fraction falling, the run-through as a fraction past 1 on home → first — through `BroadcastHud.Scorebug.Runners` and `Baserunning.DiamondPip(from, to, u)`; a seated runner is fraction 0, on the bag. The selected runner keeps the larger pip; one pad and two draw the same.

A steal departure before catcher possession keeps the normal plate/mound view and opens a race inset at the shared `BroadcastHud.StealInset` anchor. At catcher possession or a pitcher throw, the main view uses the ordinary live-play camera: `diamond` follows the dirt under the ball from possession through transfer, flight and reception. Steals do not choose a separate angle, tracking speed, lens, or forced whole-race frame. Close plays and rundowns use the same beat rules and camera hold as other live plays. The pre-pitch inset alone uses `steal-race` and its subject bounds.

The charge begins on the accepted pitch-button press. A pre-charge right-stick target plus RT is read as a pitcher throw before it can start charging. Selecting a base while holding a committed pitch attempts the step-off and shows the typed BALK result. Pitch-flight steering cannot retarget a catcher throw; right-stick selection and a fresh RT press feed its buffer. Throw animations sample the existing release marker at the sim's preparation boundary; ball attachment and YOU ownership follow that boundary.

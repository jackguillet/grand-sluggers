# 14. Parks in play

The target contract is §0.3 (D21). This section is what the code does at `d0c6e12c`, from the [sim map](../research/fields-code-map-sim.md) §3. Harbor has no hazard. ✅

**What a park changes today.** Its three fence posts, `fenceHeightFt`, `windMph`, `windDeg`, and its `hazards` — by day, and in its optional `night` block at night (§6.1, `Models.cs:186-211`; F4-d #895: the night window `nightContactWindowMul` is gone on both roots, FD-11-R2). `surface` is read by the validator and by presentation only; it changes no play. ⚠️ D21 gives it a meaning through ground zones.

**How a hazard is decided today.** `ParkHazards` runs once, in `FieldingResolver.Preview`, against the ball's **landing point**, for the ball redirect, the reward target and the catch stealer. ⚠️ D21: hazards act live on the ball or the body that touches them. ✅ F4-b (#896, FR-07): the **status volume** is live — each frame, before anybody moves, the live ball tests every fielder and every live runner where the last frame left it against the park's volumes (`ParkHazards.StatusVolumes`, `BodySlows`); nothing reads a landing point to slow a body.

**What decides which rule runs.** ✅ F4-a (#847, FD-09, FR-08): a **closed pattern library**. `data/rules/hazards.json` carries one authored row per hazard type — the twelve ids of `HazardType`, the spelling a park file uses — and each row names its **pattern** from the closed set `statusVolume | ballRedirect | rewardTarget | catchStealer | wallTrait | decoration` (`HazardPattern`) plus the numbers that are the type's own.
`ParkHazards` dispatches on the row's pattern; no method in it spells a hazard type, and a type that fits a pattern is a row in that file and **no sim code**. The shape is the pitch-family library's (D20): an id outside the library and an id inside it with no authored row are different mistakes and neither one loads, and the park validator takes its set of legal types from the table rather than keeping one of its own (`SF-03`, `HazardLibraryTests`). The numbers moved at today's values (FR-06):
`fielding.park.emberNightFireMul` 1.6 is `fireBreath.nightRadiusMul`, and `fielding.park.pipeReachPadFt` 8 (5.6 on `trials/c80`) is `warpPipe.reachPadFt` and `barrel.reachPadFt`. What a volume costs a body stays `fielding.chase.frozenMul` and the sign's payout stays `stars.gains.billboard`, because a star swing sets the same slow and the specials are outside this phase. **The library moved the dispatch and nothing else**:
`HazardLibraryTests` carries the pre-#847 type-string code verbatim as an oracle and compares the two over every hazard in every park, by day and by night, including the warp's seeded draw.

**Freeze volume, lava pit, fire breath (statusVolume), What happens today.** ✅ F4-b (#896; FR-07, FD-08-R2): **live, per body**. A body — fielder or runner — whose position at the start of a frame is inside the disc (the instance's radius × the row's `nightRadiusMul` at night) and was not at the last read has touched it: it runs at `fielding.chase.frozenMul` (0.45, unchanged, "3. keep.")
for the row's `slowSec` (**3.0 s**, Jack's number on both roots, not yet played; `[Positive]`, authored by every `statusVolume` row and by no other) from the touch. Staying inside keeps it slowed; leaving lets the time run out; entering again starts the 3 s again (a later touch never shortens a time already running). The time is counted in frames of the play clock: a frame that starts inside it is slowed through its end.
Every step the body takes is × 0.45 — the chase, the carry, the cover, cutoff and backup walks through `StepTo` / `StepStick`, and a runner's speed along his path (`RunnerTickContext.SpeedMul`); a lunge, a hand-off coast and the buddy's hover are not steps. No other body is slowed. A Burrow body (`FieldAbilities.IgnoresParkSlow`) touches nothing, fielding or running.
Each touch is a typed live fact, `BodySlowed` (the body's position or `runner`, the character, the instance's index in `Park.Hazards`, its type, the touch's play second and the second the slow runs to), raised as `LiveEvent.BodySlowed`, kept on `LivePlaySystem.Slows` / `SlowsThisPlay`, and marked in the play trace (`PlayTraceMarkKind.BodySlowed` with `PlayTraceHazard`; `Slowed` on each slowed trace fielder and runner). The preview no longer reads a volume:
`FieldingPreview.Frozen` is the heart swing's alone, and the preview plans at full speed. The route around a volume is the CPU's (below). A runner meets a catalog volume only by leaving his path, which the runner model never does (FD-19-R1 keeps every volume off the lanes), so the runner's slow is proved on a fixture. `StatusVolumeTests` (`SF-20`), `ParkSlowRowsTests` re-authored to FD-08-R1 / R2.

**Warp can, barrel, chomper (ballRedirect), What happens today.** **Live** (FR-07, FD-08-R1, FD-09-R2): a ball inside the disc (`radius +` the row's `reachPadFt`) between the row's `mouthFloorFt` (absent: the ground) and `mouthFt` enters the mouth and comes out of another instance of its type, drawn from the match's seeded stream (`Match.DrawIndex`; a mouth is never its own exit, so a type with one instance redirects nothing).

**Where a hazard may stand.** ✅ F4-e (#862; FD-19 B, FD-19-R1; `SF-23`). The content validator — so `cli art` and every `ContentCatalog.Load` — refuses a hazard whose disc crosses one of the four running lanes (home–first, first–second, second–third, third–home), the mound-to-plate lane, a bag pad, the mound or the plate area, on both data roots, and names the park file, the hazard's index and type, and every piece of ground it crosses with the depth in feet, deepest first (`HazardPlacement`,
`ContentDataValidator`). **No new number**: a lane is `ParkDiamond.PathWidth` (10 ft) wide on the line from bag to bag and from the rubber to the plate; a pad is `ParkDiamond.BagPadR` (12 ft) round first, second and third; the mound is `ParkDiamond.MoundR` (the `float` 9.2 ft the hill has always stood at) round the rubber; the plate area is `ParkDiamond.HomePackedR` (18 ft) round the plate.
Those are feet of body and equipment and do not scale; where the bags and the rubber stand is the root's own `infield.json` (`cornerFt`, `secondFt`, `moundFt`), so `trials/c80` is measured on its 80-ft diamond. **The disc is the hazard's own radius**, the thing that stands where a runner or a fielder would meet it. A `ballRedirect` row's `reachPadFt` is not counted:
it is how near a *ball* must land for the can to take it (#732), not ground a body stands on, and FD-19 keeps shallow ball hazards legal. **The disc is the one the instance plays, by day and at night** (✅ F4-d #895, map finding 31): a day instance stands at night too, so it is measured at the larger of its radius and its night disc (`ParkHazards.NightDiscFt`, its row's `nightRadiusMul`), and a night-block instance at its night disc; where the two differ the refusal names both.

**The CPU** sees a park through the clipped flight and the wall clamp, and it reads the played park's status volumes (FD-14, `SF-26`). There is no play-wide slow flag for it to read, and the fielding preview and the meet point still plan at full speed.

*The route cost of a status volume.* Every step a body takes to a goal — the CPU's chase, cover, cutoff and backup walks, and the assistance that walks the human seat's glove while the stick is neutral — goes through `VolumeRoute`. The stick is never steered. Each frame the route finds the first volume the straight line from the body to its goal meets. The disc it keeps off is the volume's disc plus `fielding.chase.volumeClearFt` (2 ft, not tuned, on both roots), so a path bent along the rim is not clipped by the body's own ramp. It compares two costs in seconds at the step's asked speed `v`:
- **Through:** from the entry the body is slowed for the row's `slowSec` or for as long as it is inside, whichever is longer, never past the goal. Each slowed foot costs `(1 / frozenMul − 1) / v` more than a free one.
- **Around:** the shortest path that keeps off the disc (tangent, arc, tangent, on the shorter side) less the straight line, over `v`.

The body goes around when around costs less, heading along the tangent. Otherwise it goes straight and takes the slow. It goes straight, with the goal unchanged, when no volume is in the way, when the goal is inside the kept-off disc (the ball lies in the volume), when the body already stands in the volume, for a Burrow body, and at a park with no volume, so every such step is the same doubles as before. The route reads geometry only:
no draw, no stream, nothing a hazard has not yet done (FD-14), so the same frame always steers the same way. At 0.45 and 3 s a small volume is almost always cheaper to go around. A runner keeps his path; the volumes are off the lanes (FD-19). The human seat's assistance and the CPU use the one route, so from the same positions both seats make the same play (`VolumeRouteTests`). Where a volume stands, the route changes play:
at Crystal and Ember a seeded game can differ from the game without the route. ⚠️ D21 (FD-14): solid bodies (F4-f), the CPU runner's margins, the CPU batter and the outfield depth still read no park field.

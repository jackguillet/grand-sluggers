# 12. Chemistry, stars, items in play

Mechanics are in systems.md. The play contract:

- **Chemistry throw** modifies throw speed and accuracy (§8.5). That is its only in-play effect. A bad throw *looks* bad before it lands (muddy trail) so the room can yell.
- **Buddy Jump / Buddy Throw** are verbs with windows (§8.4, §8.7), not rolls.
- **Stars setting:** Stars default on. Off, both teams start with zero Stars and cannot gain or receive Stars; released special requests use the ordinary pitch or swing without a cost. The HUD hides both teams' Star meters and the unavailable-special tell while Stars are off. When Stars are on, empty meters remain visible. The same match-level setting governs both human seats and the CPU.
- **Star pools** (PH-16-R4, R6, R16): each team has **one** Star pool, used by its Star Pitches and its Star Swings alike, and never by the other team. Both pools start at `stars.json` `startingReserve` 3, the same for both teams whatever they drafted: chemistry does not set it. The reserve is granted once, when the match is made. A half, a pitcher swap or a role change does not grant it again.
  The rules validator holds it inside the meter and at or above the cheaper price, so a special is affordable from each side's opening plate appearance. At 3 the reserve buys any captain's special at the opening plate appearance, a guest captain's (3) included.
- **Base gain** (PH-16-R5): when a plate appearance completes (strikeout, walk, hit-by-pitch, or a batted ball that ends the appearance, including a home run or a ground-rule double), **both teams** gain `gains.plateAppearance`, once, each into its own pool, whoever won the appearance. `Match.NextBatter` is the one seam every completion passes through. Pitches before the last one earn nothing.
  A half that ends on a runner out between pitches (a caught stealing or a pickoff for the third out, with the count unfinished) **does not complete** the appearance: no base gain, and that batter leads off his side's next half with a fresh count. A strikeout with a caught stealing on the same pitch completes it: the base is earned once. The bonuses below stack on the base, up to the meter max. `plateAppearance` is 0.1:
  a three-inning game has about 25 completed appearances, so each team earns about 2.5 Stars from the base, under one top special, on top of its bonuses.
- **Star meter**: the pool holds at most 5 (`meterMax`), so a full meter buys one top special and one low or mid special, or two mid specials. The bonuses are events (`data/rules/stars.json` `gains`), paid to the side whose play it was: single 0.4, extra-base hit 0.8, home run 1, strikeout 0.8, out 0.35 / live out 0.4, stolen base 0.35, **double play 1**, **robbed homer 1**, billboard 1. ✅ P7: every gain and cost is read from the table; the double play (two outs on one live ball) and the robbed homer (a leap that takes a ball clearing the fence, §8.4) are events of their own.
- **Star prices**: each character has one Star Pitch and one Star Swing, separate from the three ordinary pitches. The price follows the carrier, not the special: `stars.json` `prices` sets a captain's price (**2**) and a sidekick's (**1**). A captain acting for a team they do not captain (a guest captain, or a captain swapped onto the mound) pays `costs.guestCaptainSurcharge` 1 on top, so a guest captain's special costs 3. Each price costs at least 1 and fits the meter (rules validator).
  A sidekick carries only a generic special (`kind: generic`); a captain carries their own, and no two characters share a captain's special (content validator). There are no tiers: a row that names one is refused.
- **Asking for a special**: hold LT when the accepted RT pitch/swing release occurs. Both seats use their own controller. The request is sampled at release, can be removed by letting go before release, and cannot alter an already committed action. Costs, ordinary fallback and red insufficient-Star tell remain unchanged. LB advances/steals runners independently before and after contact; it is never a Star modifier.

- **Settling a special** (PH-16-R3, R12): `Match` settles every released Star Pitch and Star Swing at the release, before the flight, the stamina, the bat or the log read it. Affordable (the team's pool ≥ the price), the team pays the full price then. A Star Swing that then misses has already paid its full price: there is no miss discount and no refund. Unaffordable, the release is the **ordinary action at the same timing**:
  the pitch is the ordinary pitch of the family already selected, with its aim, charge and break; the swing is the ordinary swing with its timing and charge. No Stars are spent, and nothing upgrades the action later. Either way the play's `PlayOutcome.Stars` carries a typed `StarRequest` (action, team, character, ability, price, the pool before, afforded or not); an unafforded one is the "special unavailable" event presentation shows.
  A take with the star held releases no swing and settles nothing. The CPU asks only for what it can pay.
- **Specials keep the charge tradeoffs** (PH-16-R13): a charged Star Pitch gains the family's charge speed and loses steering exactly as the ordinary pitch of that family does (the special's speed multiplier and shape apply on top); a charged Star Swing narrows the oval exactly as an ordinary swing does, and the ability's exit multiplier multiplies the charged exit.
- **MVP** (`stars.json` `mvp`, `Match.Mvp`): a **walk-off hit** names its hitter ahead of the points (the last event of the game, a hit in the home half that turned a tie or a deficit into the lead);
  else the most points on either roster — HR 10, winning pitcher 5 (the arm of the side that last took the lead, credited once the game is over), go-ahead RBI 5 (on top of the RBI), robbed homer / buddy jump 5, K 3, RBI 3, hit / walk / HBP / SB 1, close play won 2 (the runner called safe or the glove that tagged, §9.6), item that mattered 2 (it landed and the batter reached), **putout 1** (the glove that forced, tagged, or caught a runner live, the catcher on a caught stealing).
  The "took over the diamond" / "kept the line moving" lines read `mvp.tookOverAt` 8 / `mvp.keptMovingAt` 4. ✅ P7 (no literal in `Match.cs`; a run scored is not a point of its own).
- **Items are dormant** (PH-16-R15). The on-deck item offer is removed: a good-chemistry on-deck hitter no longer offers an item, and nothing else does, so no item is thrown in a game (S-145).
  The item code and data stay, not deleted — `ErrorItems`, `batting.items`, `Match.ApplyOffenseItem` / `ThrowItem` / `CpuWouldThrowItem`, `LivePlaySystem.LandItem` / `TickItems`, the tutorial `item-effect` objective and the clients' item controls — and wake only when a new item source sets `AtBatResult.ChemistryItemOffered`. The item lessons (T-X01, T-I-banana, T-I-rocket, T-I-pow) are blocked until an item source exists. The rule below is how an item plays **if** one is offered.
- **Items** after contact (banana / rocket / POW): each is a *field* effect with geometry and seconds (`batting.items`), thrown at a body and landing after `flySec` 0.9 s of play time — a **banana is a peel on the grass** at the aimed body's feet where it lands, lying there `peelSec` 6 s, and *any* body inside `peelRadiusFt` 5 of it slips for `slipSec` 0.8 (the aimed body first, since it is standing on it);
  a **rocket dazes the body it was aimed at** for `dazeSec` 0.8 when it lands (North smashes it in the air, the only defense); a **POW keeps every ball on the dirt** hopping, unscoopable, for `powHopSec` 0.8. They add time; the geometry then decides the play. A body foiled while holding the ball drops it loose. No roll decides an item:
  the **CPU offense throws its offered item when it would matter** — when the batter's slack at first against the glove's throw from the landing is under `cpuThrowMarginSec` 0.3 (the §9.9 read) — a POW with a runner on and the ball on the dirt, a peel at the glove going for a ball on the dirt, a rocket at the body under a ball in the air (`ErrorItems.CpuPick`). ✅ P7 (`LivePlaySystem.LandItem` / `TickItems`; the 40% auto-throw roll and the rocket's daze roll are gone).

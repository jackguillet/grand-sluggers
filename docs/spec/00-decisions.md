# 0. Principles

1. **Sim owns baseball. Unity presents.** Every out, safe, strike, ball, foul, and advance is decided in `GrandSluggers.Sim` from positions and times. Unity may animate a verdict; it may not produce one. ✅ P0: `LivePlaySystem` owns the live ball from contact to Time — gloves, catches, throws, the relay chain, the close-play race, the bobble, and the steal phase — from one `Tick` command per frame carrying both pads; `InPlayDirector` translates the pads, mirrors the state, and plays the cues.
2. **Geometry decides. Rolls only add noise, never outcomes.** A stat changes speed, range, window, or accuracy. It does not roll "out or single" ✅. Randomness is allowed on *inputs* (a CPU's timing error, a bad-chemistry throw's lateral error), never on *results*. A park hazard's surprise is an input of this kind (D21, FD-08-R1):
   a seeded draw may decide when a hazard fires, which exit it picks or where it sends the ball; it never awards an out, a hit, a drop or a catch. ✅: the park's `fielding.drops.frozen` roll is retired — a glove a status volume slowed is decided by the glove and the ball (`SF-22`); the heart swing's use of the table is a special's and is unchanged.
3. **One clock, one body.** A runner has one position and one speed. A throw has one duration used both to fly the ball and to judge the bag. A fielder has one speed whether a human or CPU holds the stick. ✅ One `bagSec` for every runner; one `InPlay.ThrowSec` (the catcher's gun and Unity's throw flight read it); one glove speed for both seats.
4. **The user owns the verb; CPU covers the rest.** Dead stick means the CPU plays that seat *by these same rules*. A human never gets an auto-out and never gets robbed of one by a script.
5. **Every play type is a scene with a name.** Grounder, chopper, liner, pop, fly, wall ball, homer, bunt — each has a fielder, a runner rule, a throw rule, a camera, and a stamp (section 7).
6. **Rails, not patches.** A new rule lives in a named system with a `data/rules/*.json` number and a scenario test. Special-casing one play, one seat, or one captain is a patch (AGENTS.md).
7. **Two seconds of illegal physics, then baseball.** Star skills and items bend one rule for a beat. They never auto-resolve a play, blind the other player, or make a home run free.
8. **Readable at couch distance.** A stranger should be able to say why they were out. If the reason is not visible on the field (a tag, a bag, a catch), it is not a rule we ship.
9. **Every mechanic is teachable by playing it.** A Tutorials section must provide repeatable scenarios for individual actions and combined plays, and each new or changed mechanic must carry tutorial coverage. [tutorials.md](../tutorials.md) defines the contract and planned migration from existing Practice. Controlled setup/CPU behavior may create an opportunity; the human performs the skill and the ordinary sim decides the result.
   Every tutorial requires three separate successful player-owned attempts to pass. Successes accumulate rather than requiring a streak; failure preserves earlier successes. Save partial progress by lesson revision and gameplay profile. Retry resets the baseball scenario, not earned progress. CPU assistance and demonstrations earn no credit. A scenario test or a controls page alone is not a tutorial. Catalog, runner and expanded UI are implemented in preview; human learning acceptance is pending.

## 0.2 Field proportions and race calibration — D19

**The compact field is the game (3e).** The ordinary loop plays on the C80 contract below; there is no second profile. Where a decision paragraph in this section still says "trial", "the C80 copy" or "the shipped table keeps", the game now plays the C80 value, and the older number is only the value a switch takes when it is off. Specials and special statuses are outside this contract (they play as authored), and the throw-before-cover rule is unchanged.

- **The field.** 80-ft basepaths (`infield.baselineFt`), the rubber at 53.78 ft, the corner bags at (±56.57, 56.57) and second at (0, 113.14) — the 90-ft numbers × 80/90 at two decimals. The dress follows the bags: the grass diamond's half-width 44.44 ft, the dirt's back arc 81.78 ft (far edge 135.56 ft); path width, bag pads, the home pad, the mound table and the warning track are equipment and stay in feet. The infield lip is 137.78 ft (155 × 80/90), 2.22 ft outside the dirt; it stays on the basepath scale because a lip on the fence scale (108.50 ft) would sit inside the middle infield.
- **The parks.** Every fence is the full-size fence × 0.70 (Harbor 232 / 280 / 232; Canopy 218 / 265 / 223; Crystal 224 / 270 / 224; Ember 237 / 286 / 237; Funfair 220 / 273 / 238; Rooftop 223 / 272 / 225). One scale keeps each park's identity and their order. Wall heights and wind do not scale: bodies did not shrink. A hazard moves by the zone it stands in — the basepath scale inside the lip, the fence scale beyond it — and every hazard radius and capture pad (`reachPadFt` 5.6) takes the fence scale, because a radius is a trigger zone, not a body.
- **The ball.** `flight.drag` 0.0040 with the exit table unchanged: the best swing without a star (Power 10, perfect charge, full lift) carries 304 ft and clears every park through the middle; a charged perfect swing leaves every yard from Power 9 up.
- **The defence stands on the field.** The infield four stand at the full-size spots on the basepath scale ((±69.33, 64), (±37.33, 104.89)); the outfield three keep their bearing and their fraction of the fence at that bearing: (∓77.09, 175.19) and (0, 213.5). One set clears every park's wall.
- **Bodies.** Pursuit `chase = 12.4 + 1.12 × Run` ft/s (floor 4.72), one profile per body; an outfielder under a ball in the air × 0.6, an infielder × 1.0. Response law: 0.20 s from rest to speed, 0.10 s to rest. Read clocks P 0.35, C 0.45, infield 0.25, OF 0.40. Full-swing contact also holds the pitcher in delivery recovery for 0.65 s, blocking pursuit, dives, jumps and possession; bunts retain the ordinary read. Cover, cutoff and backup walk at the body's own pursuit speed from contact.
  The human seat's pursuit stick is the calibrated radial one (enter 0.20, leave 0.15). No universal East sprint; Ball Dash holders (dart, pip, jester) carry at × 1.20.
- **Catching.** A 4-ft stand-up reach for every body without its own (5 on the dirt, 6 with an airborne dive). Only the player initiates a dive, sideways relative to facing home plate; CPU and neutral-stick assistance never dive. Every dive costs 0.60 s at Field 1 (−2.5 % a point). The jump is a physical arc: 2 ft over 0.60 s, a 0.10-s press buffer. Recoil reads the ball's speed (ground 55 → 75 ft/s, air 80 → 115 ft/s); a routine pickup never errs, the awkward in-between hop is the one difficulty.
- **Throws.** Release 0.30 s, 88.89 ft/s for the neutral arm (0.90 s over 80 ft), a long-throw loss of 0.60 × ((d − R) / 80)² past R = 160 + 5 (Arm − 5). The relay is chosen by time (no forced-relay distance; the CPU relays when it saves 0.3 / 0.1 / 0 s by rung) and each human relay leg needs its own command (a 0.25-s press buffer). Laser × 1.25 on a throw home with a live runner on third only; Snap Throw a 0.22-s release at × 1.0; a bad pair throws at × 0.90 and never slants.
- **Running.** The runner clock is the anchor: a Run-5 bag still takes 2.95 s, so linear speed falls from 30.51 to 27.12 ft/s on the shorter path. A tag-up is a race read once at the catch (home 0.25 s, third 0.07 s, plus the rung's margin); the carry gates are never.
